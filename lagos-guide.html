The city in brief

Lagos is Nigeria's largest city and its commercial capital. It sits on a chain of islands and mainland districts wrapped around Lagos Lagoon, linked by long bridges such as the Third Mainland Bridge.

Locals usually split the city into the Island (Lagos Island, Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki), with business districts, beaches and nightlife, and the Mainland (Ikeja, Yaba, Surulere and beyond), where most people live and where you'll find the tech scene, markets and the airport.

Traffic, known locally as "go-slow", shapes every plan. Group activities by area and leave plenty of buffer time.

When to go

Seasons & rainfall

Lagos is hot all year. Rain, not temperature, is what changes, with a main wet season peaking in June and a shorter one around October.

The year in four parts

  • Dry season · Nov–Mar
    Sunny and humid. The most popular time to visit, especially December.
  • Harmattan · Dec–Feb
    Dusty winds from the Sahara bring hazy skies and slightly cooler mornings.
  • Long rains · Apr–Jul
    Heavy downpours and flash flooding, especially in June. Plan indoor backups.
  • Short rains · Sep–Oct
    A second, shorter wet spell after a drier August break.

Calendar

Events through the year

Dates shift from year to year, so check the organisers' announcements before you book.

  1. Feb

    Lagos City Marathon

    Thousands of runners cross the city, with a route that has ended near Eko Atlantic.

  2. Easter

    Beach holidays

    The long weekend sends crowds to the beaches and resorts east of Lekki.

  3. Jun 12

    Democracy Day

    A national public holiday, with events and speeches across the country.

  4. Oct 1

    Independence Day

    Nigeria marks independence from Britain in 1960.

  5. Oct

    Felabration & Lagos Fashion Week

    A week of concerts celebrating Fela Kuti around his birthday, and the city's main fashion showcase.

  6. Nov

    Art season

    Art fairs and gallery openings fill the late-year calendar on the Island.

  7. Dec

    "Detty December"

    The diaspora comes home for weeks of concerts, weddings and parties. Book flights and rooms early.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the questions first-time visitors ask most.

Is Lagos safe for visitors?

Most visits are trouble-free, but use common sense: travel by ride-hailing or a trusted driver at night, keep valuables out of sight and check your government's latest travel advice before you go.

Can I drink the tap water?

No. Stick to bottled or sachet ("pure") water, which is cheap and sold everywhere.

How much should I tip?

Tipping isn't required, but around 10% at restaurants and small tips for hotel staff and drivers are appreciated.

Do I need to haggle?

At markets and with informal transport, yes. Stay friendly, start well below the first price and meet in the middle.

Will my phone work?

International roaming can be expensive. A local SIM from one of the major networks is the easiest way to get data; bring your passport to register it.

What should I wear?

Light, breathable clothing. Lagos is fashion-conscious, so people dress up for dinner, church and events.
